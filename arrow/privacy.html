Arrow: privacy policy

Last updated: 29 October 2026.

Who uses it. Arrow is an app that runs on each user's own Mac. It is in testing, used by a small group of invited people.

What it reads from Google. With your permission, Arrow reads your Google Calendar: the list of your calendars and their events. It uses the read-only scopes calendar.calendarlist.readonly and calendar.events.readonly. It never adds, changes or deletes anything.

How it is used. Your events are shown in Arrow, for example in My day. Arrow's AI assistant reads them to help you plan your day. The assistant runs on the AI account you sign in with in Arrow (Claude, by Anthropic), so the events it reads are processed by that service under its terms and your account's privacy settings. In those settings you can choose whether your chats may be used to improve Claude.

Where the data goes. Calendar data stays on your Mac and with the AI service you use. Arrow's maker never receives it. Apart from that, it is not shared with anyone, not sold, and not used for advertising. Arrow does not use it to train AI models.

Keys. The sign-in keys are kept in your Mac's own keychain.

Removing access. You can remove Arrow's access at any time in your Google Account, under Security, Third-party connections. Removing it stops all reading.

Questions. Ask the person who invited you to test Arrow.

Arrow's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
